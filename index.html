The Process of Chemosynthesis

Chemosynthesis

Chemosynthesis is the process by which organisms produce food using energy from chemical reactions, instead of sunlight. Seawater seeps into cracks, gets heated by magma, reacts with rocks, dissolves minerals, and gushes back out enriched with energy-rich compounds.

Hydrothermal vents release compounds like hydrogen sulfide ($H_2S$), methane ($CH_4$), and dissolved iron ($Fe^{2+}$). These serve as the energy sources for life.

Formation of Heat & Energy

Water seeps into cracks in ultramafic rocks (rich in Fe, Mg). This produces molecular hydrogen ($H_2$), methane ($CH_4$), hydrogen sulfide ($H_2S$), ammonia ($NH_3$), and carbon dioxide ($CO_2$). These become the chemical foundation for microbial life.

Balanced Reaction Example: $$2Fe^{2+} + 2H_2O → 2Fe^{3+} + H_2 + 2OH^-$$ $$CO_2 + 4H_2 → CH_4 + 2H_2O$$ $$SO_4^{2-} + 4H_2 → H_2S + 4H_2O$$

The Biosphere

Ideal Chemosynthetic Planet

Colossal jellyfish-like "Siphons" house symbiotic bacteria that process dissolved chemicals. They glow and communicate through light and chemical pulses.

Evolution of Life

Crab-like "Lithovores" graze on microbial mats. Their mineral-plated shells provide armor and a metallic appearance.

Family Tree

Sleek predators called "Apexes" evolved without eyes, using chemical pulses to navigate and hunt.

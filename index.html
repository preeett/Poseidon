The process of Chemosynthesis

Chemosynthesis

Chemosynthesis is the process by which certain organisms produce food using energy from chemical reactions, instead of sunlight. Seawater seeps down through cracks on the ocean floor, gets superheated by magma, and gushes back out as hydrothermal vent fluid.

These vents release a rich cocktail of chemical compounds like hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) and methane (CH₄), which serve as the fundamental energy sources for all life on the planet.

The Biosphere

Ideal Chemosynthetic Planet

Poseidon is a water world, entirely covered by deep oceans. Beneath the waves, the ocean floor is riddled with cracks and ridges where hydrothermal vents gush hot, mineral-rich fluids, releasing the hydrogen, methane, and hydrogen sulfide that fuel life.

About Us

This project was created for the NASA Space Apps Hackathon. We are a team of two: a concept designer/web developer and a 3D simulation artist. Our goal was to push the boundaries of astrobiology by envisioning life beyond Earth's traditional parameters.
